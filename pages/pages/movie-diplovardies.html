Selected Projects ANTHIE STASINOU

Diplovardies

The film follows a young woman through two parallel versions of the same day. In one, she is a dancer. In the other, she is a waitress.

This is not a double life—it is double work. In a context where art is not paid and artistic persistence is not enough to survive, the protagonist’s body is divided.

She dances in order to exist. She serves in order to keep dancing. The same movement repeats in different settings. Rehearsal and shift operate by the same logic: repetition, discipline, endurance.

The body becomes a tool of production—whether it produces art or service. Economic necessity does not stand next to art; it runs through it. The dancer is not protected from labor. She funds her art with her own body.

As images blur, the distinction between artist and worker collapses. Exhaustion is shared. Movement turns mechanical. Identity liquefies.

The film comments on a reality in which artistic creation is not institutionally supported, forcing creators into a permanent state of double shifts. The outcome is not romantic—it is wear. This is not a personal failure. It is a structural condition.
